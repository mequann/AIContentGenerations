# Story: The Hobbled Horse (የተቀየደው ፈረስ)

Told by Mequannt, about himself in 8th grade. The friends in the story have agreed to it
being made into a film (see ETHICS.md, C-02). Original telling kept verbatim below.

## Original (Amharic, as told)

ስምንተኛ ክፍል ተማሪ እያለሁ ከቤቴ ወደ ትምህርት ቤት ለመሄድ ረጅም መንገድ በእግሬ ነበር፤ ሰፊ ሜዳ ነው
አቋርጬ የምሄደው። አንድ ቀን ከትምህርት ቤት ወደ ቤት ከጓደኞቼ ጋር ስንመለስ ሜዳው ላይ የተቀየደ ፈረስ አገኘን፤
"በተራ እንጋልበው" አልን፣ ተስማማን። ከዛ አኮፋዳዬን (ቦርሳዬን) እንዲይዙልኝ ሰጠኋቸው። ፈረሱን ከበን ከያዝን
በኋላ በመቀየጃ ገመዱ አፉን ሸብቤ ወጣሁት። "ሂድ" ስለው ሽምጥጥ ብሎ ሄደ። ልገታው ብል እምቢ አለኝ፤ ገመዱ እንደ ልጓም
ሊገታው አልቻለም። የሰው ጤፍ ውስጥ ይዞኝ ገባ። ለመመለስ ስታገል ጓደኞቼ "ጣለው" እያሉ ሲጨነቁ፣ ባለ ፈረሱ ከሩቅ
እየመጣ እንደሆነ አየሁ። ፈረሱ በጣም እየሮጠ ሳለ ዘልዬ ወረድኩ። ወቅቱ ጥቢ ወይም ጸደይ ስለነበር፣ ስወድቅ ቦታ
መርጬ ጭቃ ላይ ስለወደቅሁ ጉዳት አልደረሰብኝም። ጓደኞቼም ደስ አላቸው፤ አብረን እየሮጥን አመለጥን፣ ሰውየውም
ሳይመታን ቀረ። ጓደኞቼ ቢያዩት ደስ ይላቸዋል፤ ወቅቱንም ያስታውሱታል።

## English (working translation)

When I was in eighth grade I walked a long way to school, across a wide field. One day,
walking home with my friends, we found a hobbled horse in the field. We agreed to ride it in
turns. I gave my akofada (school bag) to my friends to hold. We surrounded the horse and caught
it; I tied its mouth with the hobble rope and climbed on. When I said "Go!" it shot off at a
gallop. I tried to stop it but it refused; the rope could not work as a bridle. It carried me
into someone's teff field. While I struggled to turn it back and my friends panicked, shouting
"Let go!", I saw the horse's owner coming from far away. While the horse was racing, I jumped
off. It was tibi/tsedey, the soft season after the rains, and I picked my spot and landed in
mud, so I wasn't hurt. My friends were relieved; we ran off together and escaped, and the man
never got to beat us. My friends would love to see it; they remember that season too.

## Audience and intent (proposed, to confirm)

- **Who:** the friends who were there, my family, and Ethiopians who grew up walking to school
  across fields, many now in cities or abroad.
- **Where:** phones, vertical, shared on WhatsApp/Telegram/TikTok.
- **Feel at the end:** laughter and warm nostalgia; "that was us." The friends should recognise
  the season, the field and the mud.

## Things that must be right

- Akofada (አኮፋዳ): the handwoven cloth school bag carried over the shoulder in rural Amhara, not a modern backpack.
- የተቀየደ = tied: the horse's front legs are tied (hobbled) with a rope; that rope becomes the "bridle".
- Season: tsedey (Meskerem–Hidar): green fields, yellow adey abeba, wet ground, teff standing.
- The owner is shown only as a distant, unidentifiable figure.

## Look (decided 2026-11-25)

Photorealistic, cinematic, not illustration; plus one real photo of the three friends today as the last shot (s13).
